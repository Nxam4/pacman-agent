## 1. In short

- `my_team.py` is now the 6th promoted champion (**r08cand2**). Every champion beat the one before

  27 candidate programs, of which 6 were promoted.

  on AlphaEvolve, Stockfish's fishtest (SPRT and SPSA), AlphaStar's league, and Ms Pac-Man MCTS
  research.

  Claude usage. A 5-hour run is in progress (started 13:12), and an 8-hour overnight run is set up.

### Loop upgrades on 17 Nov
- **Per-game play stats** (`arena/run_game.py`): food banked and conceded, deaths, food dropped,
  kills. The engine is not modified: the wrapper watches the real game states through the
  display hook. Check: score = banked − conceded held in every test game.
- **SPRT [0.50, 0.55], up to 300 games.** Simulated: a real 57% improvement passes 67-81% of the
  time (was 38-48%), and an equal one passes 3-4%. Both later promotions (r06, r08) were in this range.
- **Exploiter slot and league.** Every 2nd round, one builder's only goal is to beat the champion.
  If it does, it is frozen into `arena/opponents/exploit_rNN`. `ext_rush` (double attacker) and
  `ext_turtle` (double defender) were added as outside-style opponents.
- **Focus release** now happens after a round without a promotion. The old rule ("champion beats
  the baseline by 75%") was useless.
- **Robustness.**
  - The round number is taken from the database.
  - A round interrupted by Ctrl-C is redone automatically.
  - A usage limit stops the loop cleanly, and the missed analyst step runs on the next start.
- **Unattended runs.** `arena/start_loop.sh` and `arena/start_tune.sh` run from Windows Task
  Scheduler. `arena/keep_awake.ps1` stops Windows from sleeping while a task runs.
- **SPSA tuner, rewritten after a bug.** The first version's step sizes were about 10× too large
  and the parameters mostly drifted at random. Steps are now sized from a noise budget. The tuner
  also takes a time budget, plays a share of steps against the pool (PFSP), and ends with the full
  promotion gate and auto-promotion. A running loop stops it.

## 3. The Pacman project: how our team works

### Game and hard constraints
- The map is split in two halves. On your own side you are a ghost; on the enemy side you are a
  Pacman.
- A dot scores only when it is carried home. A Pacman that is eaten drops the dots it carries.
- A capsule scares the enemy ghosts for 40 moves.
- Enemies are visible within 5 cells of either of our agents; otherwise you only get a noisy
  distance (±6).
- Games last 1200 moves (300 per agent). Tournament points: win 3, tie 1.
- **Under 1 s per move.** Three warnings, or one move over 3 s, forfeits the game. Startup gets
  15 s. Python 3.8, standard library only, a single file, and no crashes.

### Architecture of `my_team.py` (r08cand2, 1339 lines)
```
create_team ── DOUBLE_ATTACK=1 ──> two HybridAgents (lane 0 = top half of the food, lane 1 = bottom)

HybridAgent (extends OffensiveReflexAgent)
  _turtle(): our lead >= TURTLE_LEAD (4) ?
     no  -> both attack their own lane (LANE_SPLIT)
     yes -> lane 0 defends with _guard (exit cover), lane 1 keeps attacking and comes back to help

OffensiveReflexAgent: the attacker
  _precompute     maze distances, home-border cells, dead ends (in register_initial_state)
  _threats        visible defenders + EnemyTracker beliefs of hidden ones
  _search         Dijkstra with a hard block (a defender can reach the cell first) plus soft costs
                  (defender proximity, belief mass x HIDDEN_W)
  _choose         food target: soft cost, cluster bonus (CLUSTER_W), carry budget
                  CARRY_BUDGET - CARRY_STEP x carrying; goes home when the remaining moves barely
                  cover the trip; capsule when a defender closes in; unlimited carrying while
                  enemies are scared
  _route          ROUTE_ON: orders a trip of up to ROUTE_K dots plus the return, when no active
                  ghost is within ROUTE_GHOST
  _escape         escape moves when trapped

DefensiveReflexAgent / _guard: the defender
  DefGeo          our side's exits, dead-end branches, trap mouths
  _guard          covers EVERY exit of the invader's region ("covering exits is the lever,
                  chasing is worth nothing"); joint_cover with the attacker as a second jaw (HELP_ON),
                  trap_move holds the mouth of a dead end (TRAP_ON), guards capsules (GUARD_CAPS),
                  keeps SCARED_GAP distance while scared
  _belief_targets with no invader visible: goes where the tracker says the invader most likely is

EnemyTracker (shared by both agents, exact HMM forward inference)
  elapse (uniform move model) -> observe (sonar ±6, sight 5 from BOTH agents, is_pacman side,
  vanished food, respawn) -> belief per enemy; most_likely(), sample(), prob_within()
```
Each choose_action is wrapped in try/except with a safe fallback move. Heavy work is precomputed at
startup. Game times in the arena are 3-24 s for a full 1200-move game, and there has never been a
timeout warning in gate games.

**Tunable constants** sit at the top of the file as `NAME = number` lines: HIDDEN_W, CLUSTER_W,
CARRY_BUDGET, CARRY_STEP, SCARED_GAP, HELP_*, ROUTE_*, TURTLE_LEAD (14 in total). The tuner sets
these. The on/off switches (`*_ON`, DOUBLE_ATTACK, LANE_SPLIT) choose the structure.

### How the champion plays (from the per-game stats)
- Against its own predecessors: about 30 dots banked and about 30 conceded per game. It is a race
  team: it attacks with both agents and defends only once it leads by 4.
- Against the baseline it concedes almost nothing (0-3 dots per game) and wins 11 of 12
  regression games.
- Weak spots, from LEARNINGS.md:
  - "standoff" maps (defaultCapture, fastCapture, tiny), where both sides bank 0-3;
  - officeCapture, where we die and drop the most food;
  - a "border ambush", where a returning enemy Pacman eats our carrier at the border;
  - only one invader is covered once we are defending.

## 4. The loop: architecture

```
                 ┌──────────── arena/db (program database: every candidate + scores) ────────────┐
                 │  parent selection, CONTEXT.md: weaknesses, inspirations, league, failures     │
                 ▼                                                                               │
 directions.txt ─► N builders in parallel (Claude Code, headless, 45 min each)                   │
   (analyst)       each edits its own copy; every 2nd round the last one is the EXPLOITER        │
                 ▼                                                                               │
          evaluation cascade (arena.py, parallel games, colour-swapped pairs, play stats)        │
            1. smoke: 2 games vs baseline, must be clean (no crash / timeout / forfeit)          │
            2. SPRT vs champion: [0.50, 0.55], alpha 0.05, beta 0.10, 16-300 games               │
            3. regression: 12 games each vs baseline, 2 recent champions (>= 30%) and the        │
               league: exploiters + ext_* (>= 20%)                                               │
                 ▼                                                                               │
          promote the best qualifier: freeze the old champion -> opponents/champ_rNN, commit     │
          an exploiter that beat the champion -> opponents/exploit_rNN (league opponent)         │
                 ▼                                                                               │
          analyst (Claude): updates LEARNINGS.md, rewrites directions.txt  ────────────────────────┘
          (curriculum focus enforced mechanically while arena/focus.txt exists)
```

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `arena/loop.sh` | the loop: preflight checks, rounds, gate, promotion, analyst, commits, usage-limit handling |
| `arena/arena.py` | the evaluator: parallel games, colour-swapped pairs, SPRT, crash/timeout/forfeit detection, play stats table |
| `arena/run_game.py` | runs one game like `capture.py` and records play stats without touching the engine |
| `arena/pick.py` | promotion gate rules (clean / SPRT decision / selection) |
| `arena/db.py` | program database, parent choice (near-miss continuation), builder CONTEXT.md, focus status |
| `arena/tune.py` | SPSA tuner: time budget, noise-budget steps, PFSP opponent pool, gate, auto-promotion |
| `arena/prompts/` | `builder.md`, `exploiter.md`, `analyst.md` |
| `arena/KNOWLEDGE.md`, `arena/snippets/` | research digest and tested code (enemy tracker) |
| `arena/LEARNINGS.md`, `arena/directions.txt` | the analyst's findings and the next round's directions |
| `arena/opponents/` | `champ_r01..r08`, `ext_rush`, `ext_turtle`, and later `exploit_*` |
| `arena/start_loop.sh`, `arena/start_tune.sh`, `arena/keep_awake.ps1` | unattended runs from Windows Task Scheduler |

**Why the statistics matter:** builders' own small tests did not replicate. Results of 55-57% on
60-100 games repeatedly came out at 45-50% in the gate. Only the gate's SPRT decides.

## 6. Risks and open problems

1. **We only know how we do against ourselves.** Every opponent except the baseline and the two
   simple ext teams comes from our own lineage. A strong team with a different style could
   expose the race strategy, which has no defender until we lead by 4.
2. **Mirror overfitting.** The SPRT is always against the previous champion, and the tuner mostly
   plays itself. The league and the regression stage limit this, but cannot rule it out.
3. **Standoff maps** (default, fast, tiny) end 0-0 or nearly so; nobody has found why our
   attackers stop banking there.
4. **Diminishing returns.** Each promotion gains less (90 → 83 → 72 → 69 → 57.5 → 57.9%), and 3 of
   the last 6 rounds promoted nothing.
5. **Contest machine speed is unknown.** Moves are well under 1 s here. Search-heavy future
   candidates must stay at about 0.5 s or below.
6. **Process risk.** Unpushed commits, and runs that depend on the PC staying awake and logged in.

## 7. Forecast

**Test round (freeze 22 Nov, 5 days left).** Push the current champion, or the tuned one if the
gate passes, today. Against starter-level teams I expect near-certain wins, based on 92-100% against
the baseline while conceding almost nothing. Against serious teams it is genuinely uncertain, because
we have never played one. My estimate is upper half of the field, but with real risk on two
things: aggressive double-attack teams (we defend late) and very defensive teams (our standoff-map
weakness). **The test round's main value is information:** the teams and maps we lose to become
`ext_*` opponents and loop directions for the final.

**Until the final (3 Dec, 16 days).**
- **Constant tuning:** the likely quickest gain. The early hint (67% after 10 steps) suggests
  10-15 percentage points of score against the current champion is plausible from tuning alone,
  before gains level off. Not yet confirmed.
- **Loop:** expect about one promotion every 2 rounds, each gaining about 55-58% against its
  predecessor. That is small per step, but it adds up: 6-10 more rounds should give 3-5 more
  promotions.
- **Biggest structural levers:**
  - defending against two invaders without giving up the attack;
  - fixing the standoff maps;
  - a joint playout search for defence (KNOWLEDGE sec. 1, 3; not yet tried as a joint search).
- **Biggest risk:** improving only against ourselves. Real outside opponents (fellow competitors'
  agents, test-round losses) are worth more than any further loop round.

**Overall:** the infrastructure is mature. It measures reliably, promotes only proven gains, runs
unattended and has stayed crash-free. The team is solid and still improving. If the test-round
feedback is used well, a finish in the upper part of the final standings is a realistic
expectation; winning outright depends on how we do against styles we have not met yet.
